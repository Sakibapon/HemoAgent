IDWG — Interdialytic Weight Gain

Fluid accumulated since last session. Becomes the ultrafiltration (UF) target for this session.

Formula
IDWG  =  weight_today  −  dry_weight
Logic in plain terms
  1. 1Subtract the patient's known dry weight from today's pre-session weight.
  2. 2The result is the fluid that accumulated since last session — this is exactly how much needs to be removed today.
  3. !If the result is negative (patient weighs less than their dry weight), set IDWG to zero and flag a warning — this likely means dehydration or a measurement error.
Decision tree
Worked example — PID-0001
Step-by-step
Weight today (Bwt)93.0 kg
Dry weight89.0 kg
IDWG = 93.0 − 89.04.0 kg
IDWG ≥ 0?Yes — use result
IDWG result → UF target4.0 kg → 4.0 L
Population mean IDWG: 2.9 ± 1.7 kg (4.1% of dry weight). [Al Maimani et al. 2021]

Why it matters

IDWG is the only measurement that directly sets the treatment goal. Everything about fluid removal — UF target, UF rate, safety checks — flows from this single number.

Qb — Blood Flow Rate

ml/min · Three-step decision: access type sets the base → adequacy metrics may boost it → patient factors can cap it.

Logic in plain terms
  1. 1Start with the access type. Catheter patients start at 300 ml/min (the pump speed that the line pressure can sustain). Patients with a fistula (AVF) or graft (AVG) start at 350 ml/min — both are treated the same.
  2. 2Boost if under-dialysed (AVF/AVG only). If last session's Kt/V was below 1.2 (target), add 50. If URR was below 65%, add 25. Both can stack. Hard ceiling: 450 ml/min.
  3. !Safety caps override everything. If the patient has heart failure (HF) or severe anaemia (Hb < 8.0 g/dL), cap Qb at 300 regardless of boosts — high blood flow strains a weakened heart.
Decision tree
QEHB NHS v3 · KDOQI 2015 §3 · DALP study BMC Nephrol 2008

Catheter → 300

Can't sustain high flow without dangerous negative line pressure. The arterial pressure (Pa) reading during the session governs the true ceiling.

AVF = AVG → 350

Both target ≥ 350 ml/min. No distinction between native fistula and graft. 400–450 achievable with good access.

HF cap → 300

High Qb raises cardiac preload. In heart failure this can precipitate decompensation mid-session.

Hb < 8 cap → 300

Severe anaemia limits cardiac oxygen delivery. High Qb stresses an already oxygen-starved heart.

Step-by-step

Qd — Dialysate Flow Rate

ml/min · Pure formula — no branching at all. Derived directly from Qb.

Formula
Qd  =  round( Qb × 1.5 / 50 ) × 50
Qd  =  clamp( Qd,  min=500,  max=800 )
Logic in plain terms
  1. 1Multiply Qb by 1.5 to get the target dialysate flow. This ensures dialysate moves faster than blood, keeping the concentration gradient strong enough for good solute removal.
  2. 2Round to the nearest 50 ml/min — machines only accept multiples of 50.
  3. 3Clamp the result between 500 and 800 ml/min. 500 is the clinical minimum for a standard session; above 800 the clearance gain is negligible.
  4. iThe 1.5× ratio is a guideline default, not a strict rule. Evidence shows Qd 300 ml/min achieves the same Kt/V as 500 ml/min, but 500 remains the standard minimum.
Decision tree — no branching
QEHB NHS v3 · CKJ 2024 (Qd 300 ml/min safe) · AJKD 2005

UF Target & Rate

How much fluid to remove and how fast — checked against two separate clinical safety thresholds.

Formulas
UF_target_L    =  IDWG
UF_rate_mlmin  =  (IDWG × 1000) / session_min
UF_rate_mlkgh  =  (UF_rate_mlmin × 60) / dry_weight
Logic in plain terms
  1. 1The UF target is simply the IDWG — remove exactly what was gained.
  2. 2Convert to a rate (ml per kg of body weight per hour) so it can be compared against safety limits that account for the patient's size.
  3. !Early warning at 10 ml/kg/h: Risk starts rising here (HR 1.22 vs <10). Use a slower UF profile — higher rate early, taper at end.
  4. !Hard limit at 13 ml/kg/h: KDOQI mortality threshold (HR 1.31). Extend session, add an extra session, or counsel patient on fluid restriction.
Decision tree
Two-threshold system
≤ 10 ml/kg/hSafe zone — proceed normally
10 – 13 ml/kg/hEarly warning · HR 1.22 · use slower UF profile
> 13 ml/kg/hHard limit · HR 1.31 · KDOQI threshold · extend session
KDOQI 2015 §4.2 · Flythe Kidney Int 2011 · Flythe CJASN 2023 (n=337,863)
Worked example — PID-0001
Step-by-step
IDWG4.0 kg
UF target = IDWG4.0 L
UF_rate_mlmin = 4000 / 24016.67 ml/min
UF_rate_mlkgh = 16.67 × 60 / 8911.24 ml/kg/h
11.24 > 10.0 → early warning⚡ 10–13 zone
11.24 < 13.0 → below hard limitno critical flag
ActionSlower UF profile recommended

What the early warning means

At 11.24 ml/kg/h this patient removes fluid faster than the lower-risk threshold. Use a front-loaded UF profile (higher rate in hours 1–2, taper in hours 3–4) and check BP every 15 minutes.

T2 — Dialysate Temperature

°C · Cool dialysate reduces intradialytic hypotension (IDH) risk by ~70%.

Logic in plain terms
  1. 1Check if this patient is at risk of IDH. Risk is present if they had IDH last session, OR if they are diabetic with low albumin (<3.5 g/dL) — diabetes causes autonomic neuropathy (poor vasoconstriction) and low albumin impairs plasma refilling during fluid removal.
  2. 2If at risk: set temperature 0.5°C below the patient's core body temperature (measured just before the session), minimum 35.0°C. If core temperature wasn't measured, fall back to 35.5°C.
  3. 3If not at risk: use the standard 36.5°C. Switch to cool at the first IDH event.
Decision tree
Tan et al. Semin Dial 2025 (26 RCTs, OR=0.35) · TEMP RCT NCT02628366 · EBPG §3.4

~70% IDH reduction

Meta-analysis of 26 RCTs: OR=0.35. Cool dialysate prevents vasodilation, raises vascular resistance, and maintains cardiac output.

Personalised (best practice)

T2 = core_temp − 0.5°C, min 35.5°C (machine minimum). Measure core temp before each session for best results.

Most effective range

34.5–35.0°C outperforms 35.1–36.0°C per subgroup analysis. Use the lowest temperature the patient tolerates.

Standard (no risk)

36.5°C for patients without IDH risk. Switch to cool at the first IDH event in a session.

dK — Dialysate Potassium

mEq/L · Rule of 7 base formula, then two safety overrides applied in order.

Base formula — Rule of 7
raw_dK  =  7.0  −  serum_K
dK      =  clamp(raw_dK, 1.0, 4.0)
dK      =  round(dK × 2) / 2   ← nearest 0.5
Logic in plain terms
  1. 1Subtract serum K from 7 to get a dialysate K that creates a safe removal gradient. The higher the patient's serum K, the lower the dialysate K, and the faster potassium is removed.
  2. 2Override 1 — hypokalemia: if serum K is already low (<3.5), add 1.0 to dK to slow removal and prevent a dangerous further drop. Low K has the same mortality risk as high K.
  3. !Override 2 — arrhythmia floor: if this patient has arrhythmia history and the formula gives dK < 2.0, raise it to 2.0. dK below 2.0 is linked to sudden cardiac arrest in at-risk patients.
Decision tree
DOPPS (n=55,183): dK 2 vs 3 mEq/L — no clear mortality difference. The danger is a steep K gradient, not the absolute dK value.
Rule of 7: Renal Fellow Network 2008 · Pun & Middleton JASN 2017 · PMC8767120

dCa — Dialysate Calcium

mEq/L · Four-way decision based on serum calcium level and IDH history.

Logic in plain terms
  1. !Low serum Ca (<8.5): set dCa = 3.0. Dialysate Ca is now higher than serum Ca, so calcium flows into the patient by diffusion — repleting low levels.
  2. !High serum Ca (>10.0): keep dCa = 2.5. Do not add more calcium — dCa ≥ 3.5 is associated with 2.2× all-cause mortality.
  3. 3IDH history: set dCa = 3.0 (clinical heuristic, not a formal guideline). Higher dCa improves myocardial contractility and BP stability during fluid removal.
  4. 4Default: dCa = 2.5 (KDOQI/KDIGO standard). Near-neutral calcium balance for stable patients.
Decision tree
KDIGO 2017 CKD-MBD §4.1 · Korean registry 2019 · PMC7645386

dCa = 2.5 (default)

Neutral Ca balance. Safe for most patients with normal serum Ca and no IDH history.

dCa = 3.0

Positive Ca balance — Ca flows in. For hypocalcaemia or IDH-prone patients.

dCa ≥ 3.5 — avoid

2.2× all-cause mortality in incident HD patients. Not used in this system.

Threshold is 8.5

KDIGO 2017 defines hypocalcaemia as serum Ca < 8.5 mg/dL (not 8.4 — a common error in older systems).

Heparin Protocol

Binary decision — standard anticoagulation vs heparin-free, based solely on bleeding risk.

Logic in plain terms
  1. !If bleeding risk is present (recent surgery, active GI bleed, etc.), use heparin-free protocol: flush the extracorporeal circuit with saline every 15–30 minutes and maintain high Qb to prevent stasis and clotting.
  2. 2Otherwise, standard heparin: a bolus at the start primes the circuit, a continuous infusion throughout maintains anticoagulation, and the infusion is stopped 45 minutes before the end to allow clotting factors to recover before needle removal.
  3. 3Bolus calculation: 25 IU per kg of dry weight, rounded to the nearest 100 IU, clamped between 1,000 and 5,000 IU.
Decision tree
PMC4088960 · ERA-EDTA · Daugirdas Handbook §14 (2015)

Bolus (25 IU/kg)

Given at session start to prime the circuit. Rounded to nearest 100 IU. Clamped 1,000–5,000 IU. Literature consensus: 25–30 IU/kg.

Infusion (1,500 IU/hr)

Continuous anticoagulation throughout. Clinical range: 1,000–2,000 IU/hr. Adjust for circuit clotting or bleeding signs.

Stop time (−45 min)

Stopped 45 min before session end (range: 30–60 min). Allows clotting factors to recover before needle removal.

Heparin-free

Saline flush q15–30 min + maintain high Qb to reduce stasis. Monitor circuit closely for fibrin deposits.

Dialyzer Selection

BSA-based 4-tier system. Larger membrane surface area improves clearance and reduces mortality.

Step 1 — Body surface area
BSA  =  sqrt( height_cm × dry_weight_kg / 3600 )   [Mosteller 1987]
Logic in plain terms
  1. 1Calculate the patient's body surface area (BSA) using the Mosteller formula. BSA accounts for both height and weight — a better proxy for metabolic waste generation than weight alone.
  2. 2Select the dialyzer tier from the BSA value. Larger BSA → larger membrane surface → more clearance per minute.
  3. !If the previous session Kt/V was below 1.1 (severely under-dialysed), upgrade one tier to increase clearance surface.
Step 2 — BSA tier table

BSA 1.5–1.7 m²F160NR1.6 m²Standard
BSA 1.7–1.9 m²F180NR1.8 m²Large

Step 3 — Adequacy upgrade decision tree
Mosteller 1987 · Sci Reports 2021 (n=234,638) · KDOQI 2015 §2

n=234,638 cohort evidence

Hazard ratios vs medium dialyzer:
Small → HR 1.15 · Large → HR 0.89 · XL (≥2.0 m²) → HR 0.75. Larger surface reduces mortality independently of Kt/V.

Why BSA, not weight?

BSA accounts for both height and weight. A taller, leaner patient and a shorter, heavier patient at the same weight have different metabolic demands and need different sizes.

All high-flux polysulfone

Removes both small molecules (urea) and middle molecules (β₂-microglobulin). Gold standard for biocompatibility. [KDOQI 2015 §2]

Kt/V upgrade

If prev_Kt/V <1.1 (severely under-dialysed), one tier upgrade provides more membrane surface to improve clearance without changing session time.

Clinical References

Evidence base for all 9 prescription decision rules.

[1] KDOQI 2015 — NKF Clinical Practice Guideline

spKt/V ≥1.2 target; min Qb 200 ml/min; UF >13 ml/kg/h associated with cardiovascular mortality; high-flux dialyzer recommended.

[2] Flythe et al. Kidney Int 2011

Rapid fluid removal independently associated with all-cause and cardiovascular mortality. Established the 13 ml/kg/h threshold.

[3] Flythe et al. CJASN 2023 (n=337,863)

HR 1.22 in the 10–13 ml/kg/h range (early warning); HR 1.31 above 13 ml/kg/h (hard limit). Basis for the two-threshold system.

[4] QEHB NHS Chronic HD Guidelines v3

Target Qb >350 ml/min for all; 400–450 in good access. Qd = 1.5× Qb. Reduce only for significant cardiac disease.

[5] Tan et al. Semin Dial 2025 (26 RCTs)

Meta-analysis: OR=0.35 (95% CI 0.28–0.46) for IDH reduction. 34.5–35.0°C most effective subgroup. Basis for T2 rule.

[6] NCT02628366 — TEMP RCT

Personalised T2 = core_temp − 0.5°C; minimum floor 35.5°C. Best-practice protocol for IDH prevention.

[7] Pun & Middleton JASN 2017 (DOPPS, n=55,183)

dK 2 vs 3 mEq/L — no clear mortality difference. Danger is the steep K gradient, not the absolute dK value.

[8] KDIGO 2017 CKD-MBD §4.1

dCa range 2.5–3.0 mEq/L; individualise to CKD-MBD status. Hypocalcaemia threshold: serum Ca <8.5 mg/dL. Evidence grade 2C.

[9] PMC4088960 — Heparin review

[10] Sci Reports 2021 (n=234,638)

Dialyzer surface area predicts mortality: small HR 1.15, medium reference, large HR 0.89, XL (≥2.0 m²) HR 0.75.

[11] Mosteller RD. NEJM 1987

[12] Al Maimani et al. OJNEPH 2021
