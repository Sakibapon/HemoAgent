Extracorporeal Circuit Pressures
Arterial (Pa) and Venous (Pv) Line Pressures
Pa is pre-pump (negative); Pv is post-dialyzer (positive). Blood pump ramps to 350 ml/min by min 10; Pv drifts up 15 mmHg over the session from progressive membrane fouling.[3,6] Pa alert threshold: below -250 mmHg (haemolysis risk).[3] Pv alert: rise of 25 mmHg or more from baseline (VND definition).[6]
Transmembrane Pressure (TMP)
TMP = 1.45 x Pv - (Pu + Pd) / 2 (Exorenal proprietary formula).[7,8] Slow rise from protein cake deposition on the membrane. Alarm threshold: 300 mmHg. Stable session stays 50-125 mmHg throughout.[7]
Dialysate Pressures — Upstream (Pu) and Downstream (Pd)
Pu and Pd define the dialysate-side transmembrane gradient. Both rise slowly with fouling.[7] Conductivity alarm: CD1 or CD2 more than 5% out of range triggers dialysate bypass — blood pump continues.[2,5] Pu alert above 88 mmHg.
UF Pump Encoder — Fluid Removal Rate
Upump encoder pulses/sec are proportional to UF rate. Calibration: 20 pulse/sec corresponds to 16.67 ml/min at steady state. Front-loaded profile: higher rate in hours 1-2, tapered in hours 3-4. Total removed: 4,000 ml. Amber band = early-warning zone 10-13 ml/kg/h (HR 1.22).[1,2]
Dialysate Composition and Fluid Balance
Outlet Conductivity (CD2)
Target 13.91 mS/cm (raw x100 = 1391). Alarm at plus or minus 5%.[5] Stable throughout confirms correct Na/electrolyte balance being maintained.[9]
Dialysate Temperature (T2)
Prescribed 36.5 C (standard — no IDH risk). Albumin = 3.5 g/dL sits at the threshold — condition for cool dialysate is strictly less than 3.5 in DM patients.[4] Alarm: below 35 C or above 38 C triggers dialysate bypass.
Cumulative Fluid Removed (Load Cell)
Load cell measures effluent bag weight. Value at min 50 = 1,730 g = 896 g bag tare + 834 g accumulated UF.[10] Linear slope = UF rate. Target 4,000 g by session end. No plateaus — no IDH interventions required.
